# Title and Thumbnail Guide

How to create the YouTube packaging for each book video: up to three thumbnails, up to three titles, and the supporting promotion elements (description, chapters, tags, hashtags, pinned comment, Shorts and end screens). The method was developed on Goethe's *Faust*.

## 1. Principles

1. **Honest hooks.** The thumbnail and title promise only what the video delivers. YouTube's Test & Compare picks the winning variant by watch time per impression, not raw clicks, so packaging that attracts the right viewers beats packaging that attracts the most. Misleading packaging raises clicks and lowers retention.
2. **Intrigue, not alarm.** The hook is a question, paradox, tension or striking image from the book itself. No shocked faces, red arrows, circles, all-caps shouting, emojis, "SHOCKING", "you won't believe", or invented drama.
3. **Title and thumbnail work as a pair.** They carry different information and complete each other. The thumbnail shows or asks; the title names and explains. Never repeat the same words in both.
4. **Recognisably the channel.** Every thumbnail is in the channel's colored-pencil style, so a viewer scrolling past learns to recognise it.
5. **Readable at stamp size.** Most viewers see the thumbnail on a phone, roughly the size of a postage stamp. If it doesn't read at that size, it doesn't work.

## 2. Finding the hooks

Every hook comes from material already gathered for the scenario (Book Video Scenario Guide, step 1). Before designing anything, list candidates from these seven sources:

| Hook type | Where it comes from | Faust example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The dilemma** | The protagonist's central choice or bargain | A man bets his soul that nothing can ever satisfy him |
| **The paradox** | The opening hook when it is a quote or bold claim, or another riddle-like line | The devil who "always wills the Bad, and always works the Good" |
| **The reversal** | The origins hook: how the author broke with expectation | For centuries Faust was damned. Goethe saved him. |
| **The striking image** | The most visually powerful scene | Signing a contract in a single drop of blood |
| **The quote** | The most famous line, if widely known | "Stay, thou art so fair!" |
| **The author fact** | The author hook | A book written over sixty years |
| **The modern question** | The reflective question from the outro | What would you trade for one perfect moment? |

Pick the **three strongest and most different** hooks. Each thumbnail option uses a different one, so a test compares real alternatives rather than variations of one idea.

**Hook test.** A good hook makes a curious viewer ask a question that the video answers. If the video doesn't answer it, it is clickbait. If no question arises, it is not a hook.

## 3. Thumbnails

### 3.1 Three option types

Generate up to three options, one of each type where possible:

| Option | Built on | What the image shows | Text |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A. Characters** | The dilemma or striking image | One or two main characters at the moment of tension, large in the frame | Optional, very short |
| **B. Symbol** | The paradox, quote or modern question | A single symbolic object or gesture, almost no background | Usually the hook in a few words |
| **C. Question** | The reversal or modern question | One character or object with a large calm area for a question | A short question |

If the book has no strong character image, replace A with a second symbol option. Fewer than three is fine if only one or two hooks are genuinely strong.

### 3.2 Composition rules

The thumbnail is a poster, not a scene. Compared with a video illustration, it uses:

- **One focal point.** One or two figures, or one object. No crowds, no busy backgrounds.
- **Larger subjects.** The main figure or object fills 40–60% of the frame. Faces and hands, if shown, are big enough to read their gesture.
- **Bolder contrast.** The strongest light-dark contrast the book palette allows (e.g. a charcoal figure against ivory, or ivory against cobalt). Avoid mid-tone against mid-tone.
- **Two or three palette colors at most** in the main areas, even if the book palette has more.
- **A clear text area**, if the option has text: a calm field of a single color, usually on the left or right third, not behind the main subject.
- **Strong silhouette.** The subject must read as a shape when the thumbnail is shrunk and blurred.
- **Keep the bottom-right corner clear**: YouTube places the video duration there.
- **Recognisable characters.** Use the fixed descriptors and reference sheets from the Book Style Sheet, so the thumbnail matches the video.

### 3.3 Thumbnail text

- **Length:** 2–5 words. Never a full sentence; never the full video title.
- **Tone:** quiet and intriguing. A question, a paradox or a phrase from the book, never a shout.
- **Case:** sentence case or small caps; no all-caps blocks, no exclamation marks except inside a real quote.
- **Book title on the thumbnail:** not required (the title already names it). If the channel uses a small series marker or book-title tag, its form is defined in Visual Style.md.
- **Typography:** typeface, color and treatment are defined in Visual Style.md. Text is always added in editing, never generated inside the image.
- **Language:** the same language as the video title.

### 3.4 Generating the image

Use the prompt assembly order from Visual Style.md, with a thumbnail block in place of the scene description:

> [Master style prompt with the book palette and motif lines]
>
> [Fixed descriptors of characters in the image, from the Book Style Sheet]
>
> **Thumbnail composition:** A bold, simple poster-like composition designed to read clearly at very small size. [Subject and action]. The [subject] fills about half the frame, with a strong silhouette and high contrast against a plain [palette color] background. Very few objects; no busy details. Leave a calm, empty area of plain [color] on the [left/right] third for text. Keep the bottom-right corner simple. No lettering.
>
> Match the attached references' drawing technique, texture, palette and character designs, not their composition.

Attach the book's anchor image and the relevant character sheets.

### 3.5 Technical specification

- 1280 × 720 pixels, 16:9 (generate larger if possible and downscale).
- JPG or PNG, under 2 MB.
- No black bars: if the generator outputs another ratio, crop to 16:9 before adding text.

### 3.6 Testing at small size

Before finalising each option:

- View it at about 160 × 90 pixels (a phone-feed size). Can you still tell what it shows and read the text?
- View it next to a screenshot of a real YouTube results page for the book. Does it stand out from the other results, and does it look like this channel?
- Blur it slightly. Is the main shape still clear?

## 4. Titles

### 4.1 Rules

- **Always include the book's title**, spelled the standard way. Add the author when it helps search and recognition (*Goethe's Faust*, *Faust by Goethe*). Use the author's name alone only for very famous works whose title is ambiguous.
- **Put the book title early**, within the first ~40 characters, so it survives truncation in search results and on mobile.
- **Aim for 45–70 characters.** The hard limit on YouTube is 100, but long titles are cut off.
- **Pair it with a hook or a search phrase**, using one of the formulas below.
- **No** all-caps words, emojis, clickbait phrases or misleading claims. Use title case or sentence case consistently across the channel.
- **Don't repeat the thumbnail text.**

### 4.2 Three title formulas

Generate one title per formula. Each pairs naturally with one thumbnail option.

| Formula | Pattern | Pairs with | Faust example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Hook + book** | [Book/author]: [the dilemma in one phrase] | Thumbnail A or B | *Goethe's Faust: The Man Who Bet His Soul on a Perfect Moment* |
| **Question + book** | [Short question from the book]? [Book/author] [format phrase] | Thumbnail A or B | *Was the Devil Right? Goethe's Faust Told as a Fairy Tale* |
| **Search-led** | [Book] by [Author]: [what the video contains] | Thumbnail C | *Faust by Goethe: Summary, Key Quotes and Life Lessons* |

Search phrases viewers actually use for this kind of video: *summary*, *explained*, *key quotes*, *lessons*, *meaning*, *ending explained*, *in 20 minutes*. Use one per title, at most.

### 4.3 Pairing for Test & Compare

YouTube Studio's Test & Compare lets you test up to three thumbnails, three titles, or three title-and-thumbnail pairs per video. Recommended use:

- Test **three pairs**, each a complete packaging idea (e.g. A + hook title, B + question title, C + search-led title).
- Don't change the packaging while a test is running. Let YouTube choose the winner, then record which hook type won in a channel log; over several books, the log shows which hook types work for this audience.

## 5. Additional promotion elements

### 5.1 Description

Structure:

1. **First two lines** (shown before "more"): the hook in one sentence, plus the book title, author and a search phrase. These lines also appear in search results.
2. **A short paragraph** (3–4 sentences) on what the video covers: plot, quotes, lessons. Mention key character names naturally (people search for them).
3. **Chapters** (see 5.2).
4. **Key quotes** featured in the video, with the translation credit (e.g. "Quotes from the Bayard Taylor translation, 1870").
5. **Links**: the playlist this video belongs to, the previous and next tale, and an invitation to subscribe.
6. **Hashtags** at the very end (see 5.4).

Write the description in natural sentences. Don't paste lists of keywords.

### 5.2 Chapters

- Take them from the Narration Script's blocks and major story turns, using the edited video's real timestamps.
- Start at `0:00`, use at least three chapters, each at least 10 seconds long.
- Name chapters as small hooks, not labels ("The devil's riddle", not "Intro").
- 6–10 chapters for a 20–25 minute video.

### 5.3 Tags

- 10–15 tags: the book title, author, book + "summary", book + "explained", main character names, genre or movement, related themes, and two or three broader terms ("classic literature", "book summary").
- Tags matter less than the title and description, mostly helping with misspellings and variants (e.g. "Göthe", "Goethe Faust", "Faust Goethe").

### 5.4 Hashtags

- 3–5 hashtags at the end of the description. The first three appear above the title.
- Order: book, author, broad category (e.g. #Faust #Goethe #ClassicLiterature).

### 5.5 Pinned comment

Post and pin the **reflective question from the outro** as the first comment, so viewers who heard "tell me in the comments" find it waiting. Optionally add a second question about the book.

### 5.6 Playlists

Add each video to two or three playlists, for example: the channel's main series (all tales), the author or literary movement (German Romanticism and Classicism), and a theme (deals with the devil, love and tragedy, quests). Themed playlists keep viewers watching across books.

### 5.7 End screen and cards

- **End screen** (last 20 seconds, over the outro): the next or a related tale, a playlist, and a subscribe element. The outro narration already invites the next tale, so link it.
- **Cards**: one or two during the video where a related tale is relevant (another book by the same author, or with the same theme). No more than one per five minutes. When the narrator links an earlier video (see Narrator.md), place a card for it at that line, and use the end screen for an outro link.

### 5.8 Shorts

Cut 2–3 vertical Shorts (under 60 seconds) from each video to bring new viewers to it:

- **The hook**: the opening hook and the narrator's promise, ending on the promise. Link the full video.
- **One quote scene**: the most powerful quote scene with its illustration.
- **One lesson**: the strongest takeaway, as a standalone thought.

Reframe illustrations to 9:16 by cropping or extending the background, keeping the main subject in the centre.

### 5.9 Captions and translations

Upload the final Narration Script as a transcript so YouTube aligns accurate captions. Accurate captions improve accessibility and give YouTube the full text of the video to understand its topic. Consider translated titles, descriptions and captions for languages where the book is widely read (for *Faust*: German).

### 5.10 AI-content disclosure

Check YouTube's current rules on disclosing altered or synthetic content when uploading. The disclosure requirement is aimed at realistic content; confirm whether it applies to the channel's stylized, clearly illustrated images and AI voiceover, if used.

## 6. Workflow per video

1. **List hooks** from the scenario material (section 2) and pick the three strongest.
2. **Design up to three thumbnails** (section 3): concept, text, image prompt. Generate, add text, test at small size.
3. **Write three titles** (section 4), one per formula, and pair them with the thumbnails.
4. **Write the description, chapters, tags, hashtags and pinned comment** (section 5), after the video is edited so the timestamps are real.
5. **Plan Shorts, playlists, end screen and cards.**
6. **Upload** with Test & Compare running on the three pairs.
7. **Log the result** when the test finishes.

## 8. Checklist

- [ ] Every hook is answered by the video.
- [ ] Up to three thumbnails, each built on a different hook type.
- [ ] Each thumbnail: one focal point, strong silhouette, high contrast, clear text area, bottom-right corner clear.
- [ ] Thumbnails match the channel style and the book's characters and palette.
- [ ] Thumbnail text 2–5 words, quiet in tone, added in editing.
- [ ] Readable at 160 × 90 pixels.
- [ ] 1280 × 720, 16:9, JPG/PNG under 2 MB.
- [ ] Every title includes the book's title within the first ~40 characters; 45–70 characters overall.
- [ ] No all-caps, emojis or clickbait phrases; title and thumbnail text don't repeat each other.
- [ ] Description opens with the hook and the book title; chapters start at 0:00 with real timestamps.
- [ ] Tags, hashtags, pinned comment, playlists, end screen, cards and Shorts planned.

## Appendix: *Faust* packaging

**Hooks**

1. Dilemma: a man bets his soul that nothing can ever satisfy him.
2. Paradox: the devil who always wills evil and always does good.
3. Modern question: what would you trade for one perfect moment?

**Thumbnails**

- **Option A — Characters (the bargain).** Faust and Mephistopheles face each other across a narrow desk; between them a parchment with a single red drop of blood and a quill. Mephistopheles' tall charcoal-and-vermilion silhouette fills the right half against a cobalt field; Faust leans in from the left. Text on the ivory area at top left: "His soul for one moment".
- **Option B — Symbol (the perfect moment).** A large hourglass in the centre of a plain ivory field, its sand frozen in mid-fall; a long, spidery charcoal hand with a vermilion cuff reaches toward it from the right edge. Text on the left: "Stay, thou art so fair".
- **Option C — Question (the devil's riddle).** Mephistopheles alone, from the waist up, on the right third, one long finger raised to his lips, against a dense cobalt field with a few ivory stars. The left two-thirds is calm cobalt for text: "Was the devil right?"

**Titles**

1. *Goethe's Faust: The Man Who Bet His Soul on a Perfect Moment* — pairs with Option B.
2. *Was the Devil Right? Goethe's Faust Told as a Fairy Tale* — pairs with Option A.
3. *Faust by Goethe: Summary, Key Quotes and Life Lessons* — pairs with Option C.

**Description opening:** "A scholar who has read every book signs a pact with the devil and bets his soul that no moment will ever be perfect enough. Here is Goethe's *Faust* told as a fairy tale: the full story, the key quotes and five lessons for today."

**Chapters (to be replaced with the edited video's timestamps):** 0:00 The devil's riddle · Goethe's sixty-year story · A wager in Heaven · The pact signed in blood · Gretchen · The great world of Part Two · Five lessons from Faust · The riddle answered

**Tags:** Goethe Faust, Faust summary, Faust explained, Faust Goethe, Mephistopheles, Gretchen, Faust Part One, Faust Part Two, deal with the devil, German literature, classic literature, book summary

**Hashtags:** #Faust #Goethe #ClassicLiterature

**Pinned comment:** "What would you give to feel that your life was truly complete?"

**Shorts:** (1) the opening riddle; (2) "Two souls, alas! reside within my breast"; (3) "Ambition has victims".
